# Product Foundations

Product Foundations describes one specific product in four statements: what the product is, what it does, who uses it, and who it is for. The method is in `reference/product-foundations.md`. Read it before doing anything below.

## Three ways to use this skill

**Explain the method.** Answer from the reference. Use the Tempo example when a concrete illustration helps. Say plainly that the four statements do two jobs: they are the product hypothesis before product/market fit and the source of truth after.

**Run the exercise with a team.** Work through the statements in order, one at a time, and finish each before moving on.

1. Ask what job the product gets hired for first. A job statement names no solution. Get it in one sentence before touching 1.1.
2. For each statement (1.1 product definition, 1.2 core functionality, 1.3 user groups, 1.4 ideal customer profile), ask the question exactly as the reference words it, write down what the room says, and read it back.
3. Test the draft against the "Done when" and "Fails when" lines for that statement. Name which one it fails and ask again. Do not tidy the wording yourself while the room still disagrees.
4. Keep the reference's rules: plain language, bullet lists where asked, only what is true today, and 1.1 read aloud to someone who was not in the room before calling it finished.
5. Return one page with the four statements and nothing else.

Disagreement in the room is the finding, so surface it instead of smoothing it over.

**Review four statements someone already wrote.** Grade each against its done/fails test. Say pass or fail per statement, quote the line that fails, and say what would make it pass. A backlog in 1.2, segments instead of user groups in 1.3, a persona instead of characteristics in 1.4, and an ambition in 1.1 are the four usual failures.

## Language

Reply in the language the user writes in. Keep the four statements in the team's own language. Keep the numbering 1.1 to 1.4 so the statements stay recognisable across sessions.
